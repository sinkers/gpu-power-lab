Power draw of a transformer training step on an NVIDIA B300

A PyTorch training step at Llama-3-8B shapes draws 95.4% of this device's 1100 W enforced limit, and the bare GEMMs inside it draw 98.3%. Measured alongside 50 synthetic runs covering per-unit attribution, numeric format, power capping and transient behaviour.

All runs

Sustained power by workload

Mean power over the measured window, 1100 W enforced limit unless stated, 50–100 Hz NVML sampling. Window lengths vary between campaigns; this affects the results and is discussed under corrections.

Measurements come from three campaigns on the same device: a synthetic workload ladder, a parameter sweep (caps, formats, per-unit attribution), and a PyTorch training waterfall.

Method

Where the 1100 W figure comes from

Every percentage in this report is against the enforced power management limit reported by the driver, not a vendor specification. NVML exposes four related values and they are not interchangeable, so the one in use is stated explicitly.

Three consequences worth stating.

The default equals the maximum on this device, so there is no headroom above 1100 W to raise into; nvmlDeviceSetPowerManagementLimit succeeds but has nothing to do. The cap sweep therefore runs downward, within the 200–1100 W settable range.

1100 W is specific to this SKU. The device identifies as B300 SXM6 AC. Other B300 configurations report different limits — the AWS instance catalogue records 1200 W for p6-b300.48xlarge — so "98.3% of the limit" is 98.3% of this board, not of every B300.

This is GPU ceiling power, not module or chassis power. NVML exposes separate GPU, memory and module power domains; only the GPU ceiling is populated on this part, and module power reports N/A. Nothing here measures what the board or the node draws at the wall. The idle reading of 182.43 W taken at the time of writing corroborates the 183.5 W measured 60-second idle run.

Primary measurement

Transformer training step, phase by phase

The workload is a synthetic transformer at Llama-3-8B shapes, built in PyTorch with random weights and random data. No checkpoint is downloaded: arithmetic volume and memory traffic are determined by the tensor shapes, not by the values in them, so weights would add a licence dependency without changing the measurement.

Model TFLOPS uses the conventional 6·N·T for a training step (2 for the forward pass, 4 for the backward) and 2·N·T for forward-only, where N is parameter count and T is tokens processed. It counts useful model arithmetic, not hardware operations, so it is not directly comparable to a vendor peak-FLOPS figure.

The complete step draws 1049.7 W, 95.4% of the enforced limit. The difference between bare GEMMs and a complete step — autograd, optimiser state, host transfer — is 32.0 W, or 2.9 percentage points. Revision 1 of this report projected a 10–30% shortfall for real workloads based on A10G inference measurements; that projection does not hold for training on this device and is withdrawn.

All five phases set the sw_power_cap throttle bit, so the device is power-limited throughout, not thermally limited: peak core temperature is 59 °C against a much higher thermal threshold. Adding work therefore does not add power; it trades clock for work within a fixed power budget, which is why TFLOPS falls from 1034 to 906 across the waterfall while power moves only 32 W.

The training GEMM phase (1081.7 W) draws more than the highest synthetic run in this set (tensor-only bf16 via cuBLAS, 1059.8 W). The synthetic workload was written to be a worst case for power draw and is not one here.

Reproduction: python3 scripts/r1_training.py --phase full --seconds 40 --warmup 25 --batch 2 --seqlen 4096 with gpu-power-runner --op observe --steady-sec 40 --sample-hz 100 sampling alongside. The sampler starts only after the workload signals warmup complete, so allocator churn and kernel autotuning stay outside the measured window.

At the 1100 W default no reported sample exceeded the limit across six duty periods from 1 ms to 2 s; peaks reached 98.6–99.0%. At reduced limits, peaks exceeded the limit in all four configurations tested, by 5% to 43%, persisting across the measured window rather than appearing as isolated samples.

Interpretation: the power controller does not appear to track a burst beginning from idle within the duration of the burst. Provisioning to a configured cap would under-estimate peak draw by up to 43% for a workload with this duty structure.

This result rests entirely on NVML's instantaneous counter. No external instrument corroborates it, and the two in-band paths disagree at the low end of the swing (next section). An out-of-band measurement would strengthen or refute it.

Disagreement between NVML sampling paths

Both NVML power paths were recorded on every sample. Across a duty sweep at the 1100 W limit they agree at the peak and diverge at the floor, with divergence increasing as the period shortens.

GetPowerUsage is driver-averaged and converges with the instantaneous counter only at a 2 s period. At shorter periods it under-reports the swing by up to 343 W. Transient figures derived from GetPowerUsage alone will be low, and consistently so.

The instantaneous swing is approximately flat at ~900 W from 5 ms to 2 s and drops to 565 W at 1 ms. The 1 ms result is ambiguous: it may indicate the load does not fully drain between bursts at that period, or that 100 Hz sampling cannot resolve it. The driver-buffered GetSamples path, not yet implemented, would distinguish these.

Under an enforced limit below the default, mean draw tracks the limit closely: 98.5% to 99.6%. The 1100 W row differs because the device is not limit-bound there — the clock boosts to 2032 MHz and the fixed work completes sooner, lowering the window mean.

Attribution

Per-unit and pairwise draw

Memory-side units draw the most: HBM streaming reaches 71.9% of the limit alone. The only pairing exceeding both components is DRAM with L2-resident traffic. Every compute pairing measured draws less than its stronger component alone, and the four-way mix draws less than any of its four constituents individually.

The harness was designed around the hypothesis that driving multiple units concurrently would exceed any single unit. That hypothesis is not supported on this device. The results are consistent with compute units competing for warp slots within an SM while the cache hierarchy and HBM controllers draw from separate budgets. That is an inference from the power data, not a direct measurement of the mechanism.

FP8 draws 81.2 W less than bf16. Narrow formats were the most plausible route to closing the remaining gap to the limit; on this evidence they do not. The ordering is consistent with operand supply becoming the constraint — a format consuming operands faster spends more of the window waiting on memory.

FP64 draws 31.6 W above idle. Double-precision GEMM is not a viable high-power workload on this device, which differs from earlier NVIDIA datacenter parts.

Corrections

Changes from revision 1

Revision 1 was based on 12-second measurement windows from a single campaign. Four of its conclusions do not survive the longer windows and additional workloads here.

  • Sustained ceiling: 90.4% → 98.3%. 12 s windows do not reach steady state. A 30-minute soak measured 94.4%, a training GEMM 98.3%. Mean draw rises with sustained load as die temperature and leakage increase.
  • "No throttle bit set" was an artifact of window length. sw_power_cap is set on the 20 s tensor run and on all five training phases.
  • The projected 10–30% shortfall for real workloads is withdrawn for training: a complete step draws 95.4%. It was extrapolated from A10G inference data and should not have been applied to this device.
  • "Instantaneous draw never exceeds the cap" holds only at the default limit. At reduced limits, peaks exceeded the limit in every configuration tested.

Limitations

What these measurements do not establish

  • n = 1. Every run is a single sample. Window length alone moved the headline figure by 8 percentage points, so run-to-run variance is uncharacterised and no error bars are available.
  • In-band instrumentation only. All figures are NVML counters. The host is a KVM guest with no BMC, so nothing external corroborates them. This applies most sharply to the over-limit result.
  • Single device, idle interconnect. NVLink and inter-die traffic contribute nothing here and are expected to be significant on a populated node.
  • Inference not measured. vLLM is installed; no serving workload has been run.
  • The ramp-shape comparison did not execute. Square, linear and exponential edges reported identical swings because the ramp modulates kernel block count while the tensor runs dispatch through cuBLAS, which the ramp does not affect. A harness defect, not a finding about ramp shapes.
  • FP4 untested. Requires block-scaling setup that is not implemented.
