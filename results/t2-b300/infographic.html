55 measured runs on a Blackwell B300. Real training reaches 98% of the 1100 W limit without trying — and when the limit is lowered, bursts blow straight through it.

Real training reaches 98.3% of an 1100 W part. The whole overhead stack — autograd, optimiser, dataloader — costs 32 W. You do not need adversarial code to reach the ceiling; a transformer will do.
Burst against a 500 W cap 143% Peak 717 W against a 500 W limit, held for hundreds of milliseconds. The controller cannot react fast enough to a burst from idle.
Why it matters

Size plant to a configured cap and you are under-provisioned by up to 43%. A GPU fleet is a controllable load — but only if the cap actually holds.

What we got wrong first time

Our 12-second runs reported a 90.4% ceiling that never throttled. Both were artifacts of runs too short to settle. Sustained load reaches 98% and does throttle.

Free efficiency

Energy-delay is best at a 900 W cap, not at the 1100 W default. The first ~18% of capping costs nothing; below that it is a real trade.
